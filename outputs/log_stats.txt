Biggest disagreement: 'Discharge time' scores r=+0.01 but

subtlety: it means a handful of extreme cycles are hiding the single
strongest signal in the dataset from anyone who only computes Pearson r.

5 of 6 tests reject the null at alpha=0.05.
Reading them in order:
 - H1/H2: the 14 cells differ in BOTH their typical voltage and their
   spread, so one pooled model is already an averaging compromise. That is
   why the companion script holds out whole cells, never random rows.
 - H3/H4: ageing is unmistakable - a random early-life cycle outranks a
   late-life one 98.2% of the time, and median discharge time
   halves from 2069s to 1032s.
 - H5: within a single cell the fade is almost perfectly monotone
   (rho = -0.96), which is the clean signal a linear model can use.
 - H6 is the useful failure: RUL cannot tell the cells apart because RUL
   is a countdown added by the dataset authors, not something measured.

==============================================================================
STEP 9  |  Conclusions that feed straight into the model
==============================================================================
1. 15,064 cycles from 14 cells, zero missing values,
   zero duplicates - but the cell label had to be rebuilt from cycle resets.
2. Not one of 9 variables is Gaussian (all reject at alpha=0.05),
   and a log transform fixes none of them -> use rank-based statistics.
3. Median 1.8% of cycles are IQR outliers and 182 cycles
   claim to last over a day -> the model must be fitted robustly.
4. Spearman beats Pearson on 8 of 8 features;
   the strongest monotone signal is 'Discharge time' at rho = +0.96.
5. 5 of 6 hypotheses reject H0; the cells differ in both level
   and spread, and ageing within a cell is almost perfectly monotone
   -> validate by holding out whole cells, never random rows.
6. 1 principal component explains 80% of 8 columns (91% in PC1 alone) -> the measurements
   are mostly one latent variable, so a LINEAR model is a fair starting point.
